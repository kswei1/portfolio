Blocky — Wayfair's Internal AI Agent

Redesigning Blocky from a chatbot into a structured, extensible main UI — starting new users with a ready-made template so they finally know what to ask.

Team Content Platform
Role Product Designer II
Timeline 2026
Stakeholders PM, EM and Engineers

Outcome

Whatshipped

A redesign on two fronts — making Blocky usable today, and structuring it for the main UI it's becoming.

1 min → 10 sec

Task Completion Rate

In early testing with ~10 people (team + a few users), creating a new block dropped from about a minute to about ten seconds.

Problem

Becoming the main UIbut still shaped like a chatbot

Blocky now lets users do in a prompt what used to be a manual process, and it's on track to become the platform's primary surface. But it was still shaped like a chatbot — and a chat box couldn't carry that weight. It broke down in three ways: it didn't teach (no guidance at the entry points, so new users didn't know what they could do and new features had nowhere to be announced), it didn't move (long back-and-forth conversations to get anywhere), and it didn't grow (the design couldn't extend to future features or the product vision).

How might we make Blocky usable today — and structure it for what it's becoming?

Process · 01

Make itusable now

My first bet was a wizard that walked users through pre-set questions, so they'd never face a blank box. In testing it made people faster — but new users still didn't know what a good prompt looked like. Guidance moved them through the steps; it didn't teach them the standard.

So instead of asking users to author a prompt, I gave them a ready-made template on first use: click a suggestion and a strong example drops in — showing what good looks like. The wizard wasn't scrapped — I modernized its question UI and scoped it to post-MVP.

Process · 02

Make ita platform

Then I rebuilt Blocky as a structured, extensible surface — a place where new capabilities and entry points can slot in and be surfaced, instead of living behind a blinking cursor. That's the bridge from "fixed for today" to "ready for the main UI it's becoming."

It also needed an identity to match. I gave it a metallic dark-purple brand that reads as "AI" and fits the Wayfair theme, plus a bespoke Blocky logo for a bit of personality. Across this work I designed the icon, the color, and the UI, and proposed the design solution and MVP.

Solution

What Blockybecame

The redesigned Blocky greets new users with a template so they know what to ask, moves them through tasks with fewer turns, and sits on a structured, extensible foundation ready for the features to come — dressed in a new brand and icon that make it feel like the main surface it's becoming.

Reflection

What I learned

Guidance moves people through a task; a concrete example teaches them the standard. A ready-made template did what a step-by-step wizard couldn't — new users learned what "good" looks like by seeing one. And the best fix for right now shouldn't cap what the product can become: solving for the user in front of me and the surface Blocky is growing into turned out to be the same job.

What I'd do differently

I'd validate the template with more real users before rollout, and instrument adoption from day one — so the win shows up in production, not just in early testing.
